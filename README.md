# Housing Project

Help the owner make a well-supported home purchase, and preserve useful methods for possible public distribution later.

**Current priority, 2026-10-02: the owner's purchase.** Work should help identify viable homes, compare tradeoffs, resolve consequential unknowns, and prepare purchase decisions. A public product is a future possibility, not a prerequisite for using the model.

## What useful progress looks like

A dated comparison of a few realistic candidate homes should explain:

- Which requirements are met, contradicted, or unresolved.
- Monthly ownership spending, cash needed, and retained reserves under stated assumptions.
- Actual household trips, relevant local services, and property-specific constraints.
- The evidence supporting each conclusion, its date, and what could change it.
- The next useful action: investigate, visit, seek a quote or document, compare an offer scenario, or set aside.

Keep hard requirements separate from preference scores. An attractive weighted total cannot settle an unverified requirement.

## Principles to preserve

- Collect evidence only when it can change a buyer decision.
- Keep facts, assumptions, preferences, and missing evidence distinct.
- Explain costs and tradeoffs in plain language.
- Use named places and cross streets where they make exploration useful; keep statistics on their actual measurement geography.
- Preserve source dates, uncertainty, and applicable reuse terms.
- Keep methods adaptable; the owner's preferences are not universal defaults.
- Consider public distribution after the method has proved useful in the purchase. No launch, platform, or business model is selected.

This public repository contains project planning and public research. Personal house-hunt records, household finances, private addresses, and credentials belong in a private workspace.
