# Start with the buyer's decision

Agreed direction, 2026-09-27: collect a field only when we can explain **which buyer question it helps answer and how it could change the shortlist**. The owner rejected detached-home percentages as a useful headline. Broad research remains useful, but easy-to-obtain statistics do not set product priorities.

The questions and examples below are a proposed working outline. They are not a fixed interview, adopted scoring formula, or commitment to a particular source.

## What the result needs to help someone decide

| Buyer question | Evidence worth pursuing | Useful result |
|---|---|---|
| Can I find a suitable home here within my budget? | Desired bedrooms, space and home requirements; current matching options where accessible; dated price evidence and itemized recurring-cost assumptions | Whether there is evidence of suitable options, the cost range and assumptions, and what remains unknown |
| Will daily life work for everyone? | Each person's actual destinations, travel mode, schedule, frequency and limits; appropriate route evidence | Each person's trips and tradeoffs, including an unacceptable trip that an average would hide |
| Can I do the things I routinely need or enjoy? | The user's relevant groceries, parks, childcare, healthcare, activities and relationships; usable entrances, routes, hours or eligibility where relevant | Concrete options and practical access to them |
| Will I like spending time here? | Direct preferences for activity, quiet, greenery, space and walking; relevant street conditions, land cover, noise evidence and human review | Specific reasons to explore a pocket, plus things to check on a visit |
| Is there something that could rule this out? | User-declared requirements; relevant hazards, insurance, school assignment, accessibility or property constraints | A supported conflict, an unresolved requirement, or a clear follow-up check |
| Where should I actually go and look? | Named places, meaningful cross streets, local destinations and reasons tied to this household | A small exploration area and a practical visit plan |

These questions apply selectively. A library matters if the household uses it. A school requirement needs the relevant program and eligibility, not an assumption that everyone wants the same school score. A two-person search keeps both people's requirements visible.

## Give every field a job

Before promoting a candidate from the inventory into collection, fill in five short statements:

1. **Question:** the buyer question, in their words.
2. **Decision:** the answer that could change which areas they investigate.
3. **Evidence:** what this field actually measures, at what location and date.
4. **Use:** evaluate an explicit requirement, explain a tradeoff, provide optional context, or prompt a visit/property check.
5. **Limit:** what we still cannot conclude; how missing or uncertain evidence affects the result.

If the only justification is “this is available for every Census area,” leave it in the research inventory. Availability helps choose a source after its purpose is established.

## Concrete examples

| Candidate | Buyer decision it can support | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Percentage of detached homes | Occasionally gives background when someone explicitly wants to explore a particular building pattern; does not identify a suitable home for sale | Optional background only. No default headline or exclusion threshold |
| Desired home's bedrooms, yard, parking or step-free access | Whether a particular home meets an explicit need | Ask directly; seek matching property evidence. Area averages cannot establish these features |
| Current matching homes and their costs | Whether the area has plausible options for this household | High-value evidence if obtainable with usable terms. An incomplete feed with no matches does not prove none exist |
| Historical area median value | Broad historical context when the user wants it | Label its date and meaning. Do not substitute it for today's price of a suitable home |
| Travel to a workplace three days a week | Whether that person's routine is acceptable | Show the relevant route/time evidence and its limits; ask only for necessary private inputs |
| Distance to any supermarket | Whether routine groceries are convenient | Establish a suitable store and usable route; a point across a barrier is insufficient |
| Housing age | May prompt condition questions if the household cares about renovation or maintenance | Background or property follow-up. Age alone is not condition or repair cost |
| Broadband subscription percentage | Does not establish whether a prospective home can support remote work | Do not use for remote-work fit; pursue address-level service evidence |
| An overall walkability score | At most background for a stated walking preference | Prefer the actual walks the household wants to make; do not let a composite replace them |

## How an answer changes the next question

For discussion: “Is there anywhere you need to get to regularly?” If yes, ask where, how often, by what mode and what feels too long. If there are several household members, keep their trips separate. If no, move to the next relevant routine. The number of follow-up questions comes from the decision, not a target questionnaire length.

A requirement can rule out a candidate only when appropriate evidence supports the conflict. Missing evidence remains unresolved. A preference explains a tradeoff rather than automatically becoming a cutoff. An area with a minority of three-bedroom homes may still contain exactly the home someone needs.

## What the Granada Hills / Ames exercise actually established

The [source exercise](worked-area-profiles.md) demonstrated that we can obtain public background data, preserve uncertainty and identify geographic mismatches. It did **not** establish household fit, suitable homes available today, actual commutes, or how either place feels to live in.

The **[home-and-budget component review](home-and-budget.md)** now defines a proposed answer, conditional questions, a fictional cost example and limits of free source evidence. Keep the broader inventory available as other buyer questions become relevant. No new collection service or website build is selected by this outline.
